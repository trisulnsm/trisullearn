# What is a Flow Collector?

A Flow Collector is a system that receives, stores, and manages network flow records exported by devices such as routers, switches, firewalls, and traffic probes.

Flow collectors act as the ingestion layer in flow monitoring environments, handling large volumes of traffic metadata generated by technologies such as [NetFlow](/glossary/netflow), [IPFIX](/glossary/ipfix), and [sFlow](/glossary/sflow).

Collectors focus on scalable traffic data collection and retention rather than deep traffic analysis.

## How a Flow Collector Works

Network devices continuously generate flow records describing traffic activity across the network.

These records are exported to a flow collector using protocols such as:
- NetFlow
- IPFIX
- sFlow

The flow collector:
- receives incoming flow records
- indexes traffic metadata
- stores flow information
- manages retention
- forwards data to analytics systems if required

A typical workflow looks like this:

1. Network devices export flow records
2. The flow collector receives the traffic data
3. Records are indexed and stored
4. A [Flow Analyzer](/glossary/flow-analyzer) processes the data for visibility and analytics

Flow collectors commonly handle:
- high-volume ingestion
- traffic retention
- distributed collection
- multi-device aggregation
- scalable storage workflows

## Why Flow Collectors Matter

Large networks generate millions of flow records every second.

Without a dedicated collection layer, traffic analytics systems may struggle to:
- scale effectively
- retain historical traffic
- process distributed flow exports
- manage high-throughput ingestion

Flow collectors help organizations:
- centralize traffic collection
- retain flow history
- scale monitoring environments
- improve analytics efficiency
- support distributed deployments
- maintain long-term traffic visibility

Flow collectors are especially important in:
- ISP environments
- enterprise networks
- SOC infrastructures
- cloud environments
- large-scale monitoring systems

## Common Operational Use Cases

### Centralized Flow Collection

Aggregate traffic records from multiple network devices.

### Long-Term Traffic Retention

Store historical flow data for analytics and investigations.

### Distributed Monitoring

Deploy collectors close to traffic sources in multi-site environments.

### High-Speed Traffic Ingestion

Handle large-scale traffic exports efficiently.

### Compliance and Audit Retention

Retain traffic metadata for operational or regulatory requirements.

## Flow Collector vs Flow Analyzer

| Feature | Flow Collector | Flow Analyzer |
|---|---|---|
| Primary Role | Receive and store flow data | Analyze and visualize traffic |
| Focus | Scalability and retention | Operational analytics |
| Data Processing | Minimal | Extensive |
| Main Output | Stored flow records | Dashboards and reports |
| Operational Layer | Backend infrastructure | User-facing visibility |

Collectors focus on traffic ingestion and storage, while analyzers focus on visibility and operational insights.

## FAQ

### What is a flow collector?

A flow collector is a system that receives and stores flow records exported by network devices.

### What types of data do flow collectors receive?

Flow collectors commonly receive NetFlow, IPFIX, sFlow, and related traffic metadata.

### Why are flow collectors important?

They help centralize traffic collection, scale monitoring environments, and retain historical traffic data.

### What's the difference between a flow collector and a flow analyzer?

A collector stores traffic data, while an analyzer processes and visualizes that data.

### Can flow collectors handle high-speed networks?

Yes. Modern flow collectors are designed for large-scale, high-throughput traffic environments.

### Are flow collectors useful for ISPs?

Yes. ISPs use flow collectors for subscriber analytics, traffic retention, peering visibility, and compliance workflows.
