# What is JFlow?

**JFlow** is a **flow export format** generated by **Juniper network devices** that sends **summarized traffic records** (flow records) to a **flow collector** for analysis and reporting. Instead of exporting full packets, JFlow exports **aggregated conversations** at the flow level, giving operators visibility into who is talking, how much data is moving, and for how long, while keeping overhead much lower than continuous packet capture.

---

## How JFlow works

On a Juniper router or switch, **JFlow is enabled at specific interfaces or zones**, where the device observes traffic and groups packets into **flows** based on keys such as **source IP, destination IP, source port, destination port, and protocol**. For each flow, the device exports a **JFlow record** that includes:

- Source and destination addresses and ports.  
- Protocol and interface information.  
- Byte and packet counts.  
- Timing (start/end or duration).  

These records are sent periodically to a **flow collector**, which then aggregates them for dashboards, reports, and historical analysis. JFlow is therefore a **compact, lightweight telemetry** source ideal for long‑term monitoring.

---

## JFlow in network operations

In **enterprise and service‑provider networks**, JFlow is used to:

- Understand **which hosts or services are communicating**, and how much traffic they generate.  
- Detect **anomalies, heavy users, or unexpected traffic patterns** that raw interface counters cannot reveal.  
- Support **troubleshooting, capacity planning, and monthly usage reports** without the storage and performance overhead of PCAP.  

Because JFlow is built into Juniper platforms, it integrates naturally into **Juniper‑centric network stacks**, especially where traffic visibility is required across core, edge, and access layers.

---

## Common JFlow fields

| Field | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Source | IP address where the flow originates |
| Destination | IP address where the flow is destined |
| Ports | Source and destination ports that indicate the application or service |
| Protocol | Transport or higher‑level protocol (e.g., TCP, UDP) |
| Bytes | Total number of bytes in the flow |
| Packets | Total number of packets in the flow |
| Timing | Flow start/end time or duration, often used for sessions and volume analysis |

These fields form the basis for traffic summaries, Top‑N reports, and alerting on traffic thresholds.

---

## What makes JFlow useful

JFlow is particularly useful because it provides **network‑level visibility with low overhead**. It avoids the storage, bandwidth, and processing costs of full packet capture while still enabling:

- **Traffic pattern analysis** (top hosts, top applications, top destinations).  
- **Usage‑based reporting** and capacity‑planning insights.  
- **Baseline‑driven anomaly detection** (e.g., traffic spikes, new destinations).  

For many operators, JFlow is the **primary telemetry source** for Juniper devices, especially when detailed but not packet‑level data is sufficient.

---

## How Trisul handles JFlow

Trisul can **ingest and analyze JFlow records** from Juniper devices to provide **traffic visibility, historical reporting, and drill‑down history**. Within Trisul:

- JFlow records are normalized into the same flow model as **NetFlow, IPFIX, and sFlow**.  
- Operators can view **Juniper‑generated flows** alongside traffic from other vendors and telemetry sources.  
- JFlow is used to generate **Top‑N charts, trends, and alerting** based on Juniper gateway or edge devices.  

This makes JFlow a natural input for Juniper‑heavy environments that rely on Trisul for unified, flow‑based network and security analytics.
